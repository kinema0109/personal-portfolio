## Goal

Draw the portrait that sits in the dialogue box: Thọ's face, from his own photograph, **wearing glasses** (he wears them; the photo does not show them). It replaces a placeholder that was never meant to survive.

## The photograph

**The owner has it and will paste it to you — ask for it before you draw.** It is a chat attachment, not a file in the repository, and it cannot be exported from the previous session. This description is here so you can check you received the right picture, not as a substitute for it:

A front-facing ID photograph on a plain white background. Short black hair with a straight, blunt fringe across the forehead, covering the tops of the ears. A broad face, warm mid-tone skin, straight dark brows, dark eyes, a small nose and a closed mouth with slightly pink lips. Neutral expression. A light blue collared shirt.

**The shirt is free.** The owner said so, and the figure sitting at the desk in the room wears a dark red sweater — matching it keeps the portrait and the figure recognisably the same person.

## The frame you are drawing into

`src/art/Portrait.tsx`. The parts that are settled and should not change:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Grid | 24 × 24, `viewBox="0 0 24 24"` |
| Rendered at | 72 screen px, so **one portrait pixel is three screen pixels** — the same as one pixel of the room |
| Drawn with | `PixelRects`, where `Px` is `[x, y, width, height, colour]` |
| Background | `C.slate`, filling the frame |
| Talking | `THO_MOUTH` is a second, open-mouth frame drawn over the portrait while a line appears; keep it aligned with the closed mouth in `THO` |

24 was chosen deliberately. The portrait was 16×16 and there was no room for a face with glasses in it — the brows, the frames and the eyes all wanted the same two rows. Do not go back to 16. Going above 24 breaks the match with the room's grid.

## What the rejected attempts got wrong

Three passes are in the history (`git log -- src/art/Portrait.tsx`); the current pixels are the third and the owner rejected all of them. What failed, so you do not repeat it:

- **The lenses were drawn edge to edge.** With the left lens ending where the right one begins, the two top rims join into a single dark bar straight across the face. The lenses need clear skin between them, and the bridge should be one row so the skin above and below it still reads.
- **The eye filled the lens.** A 2×2 eye inside a 2×2 lens interior leaves no skin, so each lens reads as a black square rather than glass over a face. Leave skin visible inside the lens.
- **The hair crossed the forehead.** Hair blocks drawn after the face covered rows 8–9, so there was no forehead at all and the head read as a helmet. The hair stops at the hairline and a single column at each temple; nothing below that.
- **Everything is still too heavy.** Even with those fixed, the current attempt reads as blocky slabs: the frames dominate, the mouth is a floating pink bar, the neck is a column. This is the part that needs an artist rather than another round of the same approach.

## Acceptance

- It reads as a face at native size, in the dialogue box, without magnifying it. Check a screenshot at 1440×900.
- The glasses read as glasses: two separate lenses with skin behind them, not a dark band.
- It is recognisably the man in the photograph — hair shape, face width, brow line.
- It carries no more pixel detail than the room around it.
- The talking frame still lines up: open the room and watch the mouth while a line appears.
